# User Stories: ACME Salary Management

**Persona:** Priya, HR Manager at ACME. She looks after salary data for about 10,000 employees in several countries. Today that data sits in a set of Excel workbooks: one per country, copied around by email, with no history of who changed what. Leadership regularly asks her questions like *"What do we pay a senior engineer in Germany?"* or *"How much did payroll grow after the annual review?"*, and each one costs her an afternoon of VLOOKUPs.

**What she needs:** one trusted place to look up and update salaries, plus answers about how the org pays people within seconds instead of hours.

Priority uses MoSCoW: **M**ust, **S**hould, **C**ould. Story IDs are referenced from `requirements.md`.

---

## Epic A: Employee directory

### US-01 Browse employees (M)
As an HR manager, I want to page through all employees in a table so that I can see the workforce without opening a spreadsheet.
- The table shows employee code, name, job title, department, country, current salary (in local currency) and status.
- Results are paginated on the server (default 25 per page, options 25/50/100) and the total count is shown.
- I can sort by name, department, hire date and salary.
- Each page loads in under 1 second with 10,000 employees.

### US-02 Search employees (M)
As an HR manager, I want to search by name, employee code or email so that I can find a person in seconds.
- Search is case-insensitive and matches partial text.
- Search can be combined with the filters in US-03.

### US-03 Filter employees (M)
As an HR manager, I want to filter by country, department, job title and status so that I can work on one slice of the org at a time.
- Filters can be combined, and pagination resets to page 1 when a filter changes.
- The active filters are kept in the URL, so a filtered view can be bookmarked or shared.

### US-04 View employee profile (M)
As an HR manager, I want a single page for each employee so that I can see their job details, current salary and salary history together.
- The page shows personal and job details, the current salary with its effective date, and the full salary history (newest first).

### US-05 Add an employee (M)
As an HR manager, I want to add a new hire together with their starting salary so that the record is complete from day one.
- Required: first name, last name, email, country, department, job title, job level, hire date and starting salary.
- The hire date cannot be in the future, and the job title must belong to the selected department.
- Email and employee code must be unique. The employee code is generated by the system.
- The salary currency comes from the country and cannot be typed in by hand.
- Validation errors appear next to the field they belong to.

### US-06 Edit employee details (M)
As an HR manager, I want to correct an employee's details (name, department, title and so on) so that records stay accurate.
- Salary cannot be edited here. It changes only through US-08, so that every salary change has a reason and an effective date.
- Country cannot be changed, because it sets the salary currency. Relocations are out of scope for v1.
- If someone else changed the record after I opened it, my save is rejected and I am asked to reload, so their change is not silently overwritten.

### US-07 Deactivate an employee (S)
As an HR manager, I want to mark an employee as terminated rather than delete them so that their pay history stays available for audit.
- Terminated employees are excluded from analytics and the default directory view, and can still be found by filtering on status.
- Records are never hard-deleted.

## Epic B: Salary management

### US-08 Update a salary (M)
As an HR manager, I want to record a salary change with an effective date and a reason so that every change is intentional and explained.
- Inputs: new annual amount, effective date, and a reason (Promotion, Annual Review, Market Adjustment, Correction, Other) with an optional note.
- The amount must be greater than 0 and different from the current salary.
- The effective date must be between the current salary's effective date and today, inclusive. Future-dated changes are out of scope for v1.
- A reason of "Other" requires a note.
- Before saving, I am always asked to confirm the change, with the current and new salary shown side by side.
- The change is atomic: the new salary record and its audit entry are saved together or not at all.

### US-09 View salary history (M)
As an HR manager, I want to see every past salary for an employee so that I can understand how their pay has changed over time.
- Each entry shows the amount, currency, effective date, reason, % change from the previous salary, and who made the change and when.

### US-10 Audit trail (S)
As an HR manager, I want every create, update or deactivation of employee and salary data to be logged with before and after values so that I can answer "who changed this and when?"
- Audit entries can only be added. The application provides no way to edit or delete them.
- Each employee's audit entries are shown on their profile.

## Epic C: Pay insights ("how does the org pay people?")

### US-11 Org overview dashboard (M)
As an HR manager, I want a dashboard with headcount, total annual payroll, and average and median salary so that I can see the org at a glance.
- Org-wide money figures are shown in USD (the reporting currency), and the conversion rates used are labelled on the page.
- The dashboard shows headcount by country and by department.

### US-12 Pay by country (M)
As an HR manager, I want min, max, average and median salary for each country so that I can compare pay across locations.
- Figures are shown in local currency, with the USD equivalent alongside.

### US-13 Pay by department (M)
As an HR manager, I want the same statistics broken down by department, optionally limited to one country, so that I can spot departments that are paid above or below the rest.

### US-14 Pay bands by job title and level (S)
As an HR manager, I want to pick a job title (and optionally a level and country) and see the min, median and max salary so that I can answer "what do we pay a Senior Engineer in Germany?" and check offers against it.

### US-15 Recent salary changes (S)
As an HR manager, I want to see salary changes made in the last N days, with the average % increase, so that I can track the impact of a review cycle.

### US-16 Export filtered list to CSV (C)
As an HR manager, I want to export the current filtered directory to CSV so that I can share a slice with finance during the transition away from Excel.

## Epic D: Data setup and migration

### US-17 Seeded realistic data (M)
As an evaluator or demo user, I want the system to start with 10,000 realistic employees so that search, filters and analytics can be tried at real scale.
- The seed is deterministic: a fixed random seed means every run produces the same data.
- Salaries are plausible for each country, department and level, and some employees have more than one salary history entry.
- Running the seed again does not create duplicate data.
- The seed can also write a sample CSV in the same format as the HR import (US-21), so the import can be demonstrated and tested.

### US-21 Import employees from CSV (M)
As an HR manager, I want to upload our existing employee spreadsheet as a CSV file so that I can move off Excel without retyping 10,000 records.
- I can download a CSV template with the expected columns and one example row.
- After uploading, I first see a **preview**: the total, valid and invalid row counts, and a list of errors by row and column (for example, "Row 214, country_code: unknown country 'DX'"). Nothing is saved at this point.
- I can import only when every row is valid. The import is all-or-nothing, in one transaction.
- Each row goes through the same rules as "Add employee" (US-05). In addition, an email must not already exist in the system or appear twice in the file.
- Department and job title are matched by name (case-insensitive), as they appear in the spreadsheets. The currency column must match the country's currency, which catches salaries entered in the wrong currency.
- An optional `salary_reason` column says why the current salary is what it is (for example *Annual Review*), when it was set after the hire date. If the salary is still the starting salary, the reason is *New Hire*; if I leave it blank, it shows as *Not recorded*.
- Every imported employee gets an employee code, a first salary record and an audit entry (*Imported*) with my username. Imported salaries are not counted as recent raises.
- Files are limited to 5 MB and 10,000 rows. UTF-8 files from Excel, including the byte-order mark Excel adds, are accepted.

## Epic E: Access

### US-18 Sign in (M)
As an HR manager, I want to sign in with my username and password so that only authorised people can see or change salary data.
- Every page and API endpoint except the login page requires a signed-in user. Unauthenticated API calls return `401`.
- A wrong username or password shows a generic "Invalid credentials" message that does not reveal which one was wrong.
- Passwords are stored only as BCrypt hashes. No real credentials are committed to the repository: the first **super admin** account is created at startup from environment variables (`APP_ADMIN_USERNAME`, `APP_ADMIN_PASSWORD`). If no password is set, a random one is generated and written to the log once.

### US-19 Sign out and session expiry (M)
As an HR manager, I want to sign out, and to be signed out automatically after a period of inactivity, so that salary data is not left open on a shared screen.
- Signing out ends the server session and returns me to the login page.
- After 30 minutes of inactivity the session expires, and my next action sends me to the login page.

### US-20 Changes are attributed to me (M)
As an HR manager, I want every salary change and audit entry to record the signed-in user automatically so that the audit trail is trustworthy.
- `changed_by` comes from the authenticated session and is never taken from the request body.

### US-22 Manage HR users (M)
As a super admin, I want to add HR managers and control who can sign in so that each person has their own account and the audit trail shows who did what.
- There are two roles: **super admin** and **HR manager**. A super admin can do everything an HR manager can, and also manages users. HR managers can't see or reach user management (the API returns `403`).
- A super admin can add an HR manager with a username, a name and a temporary password (10–72 characters), and share the password privately. Usernames are case-sensitive at sign-in, but two can't differ only in case.
- A super admin can disable or enable an HR manager. A disabled user can't sign in, and an open session of theirs ends on its next request.
- A super admin can reset an HR manager's password.
- Super admin accounts can't be disabled or changed from the app, so the last super admin can't be locked out.
